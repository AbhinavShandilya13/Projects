About The Project
This project addresses a critical business problem for a fictional bank: a high customer churn rate of 20.4%. A high churn rate leads to significant revenue loss and increased costs for acquiring new customers. The goal of this analysis was to move beyond the numbers and understand the story behind why customers are leaving.

By digging into a dataset of 10,000 customers, this project identifies the specific segments and behaviors most associated with churn. The ultimate aim is to provide the bank with a clear, data-driven roadmap to improve customer retention strategies and protect its bottom line.


Key Business Metrics at a Glance: 

A high-level overview of the customer base reveals several key indicators that frame the scope and financial urgency of the churn problem:

- Churn Rate: 20.4%

  - Business Impact: The bank is losing one in every five customers. This attrition rate represents a direct and significant threat to revenue stability and market share, necessitating immediate retention efforts.

- Average Customer Balance: $76.5K

  - Business Impact: This is the most critical financial metric. With an average balance this high, the 2,037 lost customers represent approximately $155 Million in withdrawn funds, severely impacting the bank's liquidity and lending capacity.

- Active Members: 51.5%

  - Business Impact: Nearly half of the entire customer base is inactive (48.5%). This low engagement is a major leading indicator of churn and represents a large, identifiable group for the targeted re-engagement campaigns recommended below.

- Customers with Credit Card: 70.6%

  - Business Impact: While product penetration is high, it is not a strong enough factor on its own to ensure loyalty. This reinforces the finding that customer satisfaction may be low even among multi-product customers, and the value of these products needs to be better communicated.

Key Findings and Recommendations : 

The analysis uncovered that churn is not random but concentrated in specific, identifiable customer groups.

🔑 Key Findings :

- Geographic Hotspot: Customers in Germany churn at a significantly higher rate.

- Engagement is Everything: Inactive members are a major flight risk.

- At-Risk Age Group: The 41-60 age group shows the highest propensity to churn.

- The Product Paradox: Customers with 3 or 4 products leave at an alarmingly high rate, suggesting issues with product bundling or satisfaction.

🚀 Actionable Recommendations :

1- Target Inactive Members: Launch a re-engagement campaign with incentives for inactive customers.

2- Investigate the German Market: Conduct a localized analysis to understand competitor advantages and specific customer pain points in Germany.

3- Review Multi-Product Offerings: Analyze the customer journey for those with 3+ products to identify and fix sources of friction or dissatisfaction.

4- Create Tailored Loyalty Programs: Develop loyalty initiatives aimed at the 41-60 age demographic to reward their tenure and reduce churn.
